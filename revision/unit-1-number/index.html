Revision notes — the five strands of Unit 1

1Recurring decimals → fractions

Dot notation: a dot over a digit means that digit repeats; dots over the first and last digit of a block mean the whole block repeats. 0.7 = 0.777…   0.45 = 0.454545…   2.165 = 2.165165165…

Instant rules: one 9 for each digit in the repeating block → 1 digit: 0.7 = 7/9  ·  2 digits: 0.45 = 45/99 = 5/11  ·  3 digits: 0.123 = 123/999 = 41/333
then add one 0 for each digit sitting after the point but before the block → 0.12 345… has block 345 and “12” in front, so the denominator is 999 then 00 = 99900, and the top is 12345 − 12 = 12333 → 12333/99900 = 4111/33300.
No digits in front → no 0s → that’s the plain “over 9 / 99 / 999” case.

Algebra method (use for any mixed decimal — this is the full-marks method):

  1. Let x = the decimal.
  2. Multiply by 10a so the repeating part starts straight after the point.
  3. Multiply by 10b so one whole repeating block sits before the point (b > a).
  4. Subtract the two equations — the repeating tails cancel.
  5. Solve for x and simplify.
0.4515151…    x = 0.4515151…   10x = 4.515151…   1000x = 451.515151…
             1000x − 10x = 990x = 447   →   x = 447/990 = 149/330

Traps: give the fraction in its simplest form; convert to a mixed number if the question says so; a rounded decimal scores zero.

2Percentages

Multipliers are the fastest route.

JobDo thisExample
Increase by r%× (1 + r/100)+37.4% → ×1.374
Decrease by r%× (1 − r/100)−16% → ×0.84
Percentage change(difference ÷ original) × 10070 → 98: 28/70 = 40%
Reverse (find original)final ÷ multiplier£420 after 20% off → 420 ÷ 0.8 = £525
Simple interest: interest = P × r × n  (same interest every year, on the original only)
Compound interest / depreciation: amount = P × (1 ± r)n  (interest earns interest)

Profit & loss are always a percentage of the cost price. “Sells at 80% above cost” means the selling price is 180% of cost; a further 25% off gives 180 × 0.75 = 135% → 35% profit.

Traps: round only at the end; “how much interest” is not the total in the account; VAT, deposits and “per year” rates all use the same multiplier idea.

3Degree of accuracy & bounds

Bounds = the value ± half the stated accuracy. Error interval: lower bound ≤ x < upper bound.

StatementLower boundUpper bound
105 cm (nearest cm)104.5105.5
4.2 (1 d.p.)4.154.25
0.08 (1 s.f.)0.0750.085
70 (1 s.f.)6575
40 (nearest 10)3545
Calculations: for the biggest answer use the biggest top and the smallest bottom (max: UB×UB, UB÷LB  |  min: LB×LB, LB÷UB).
For a sum, max uses both UBs and min uses both LBs. For a subtraction, max = UB − LB and min = LB − UB.

Truncation cuts off rather than rounds: 159 000 truncated to 3 s.f. lies in 159 000 ≤ x < 160 000.

“Suitable degree of accuracy”: work out both bounds, then quote the value at the accuracy where the bounds agree, and say so: “x = 4.2 to 1 d.p. as both bounds round to 4.2.”

4Ratio

Simplifying: divide every part by the HCF — and convert units first (2 km : 3400 m = 2000 : 3400 = 10 : 17). With algebra: 12a² : 8a = 3a : 2.

Sharing into a ratio: add the parts, 1 part = total ÷ number of parts, then multiply up.

The exam favourite: one part is given → find 1 part first, then scale.
children : adults = 2 : 3 with 80 children → 1 part = 40 → 120 adults.
If a difference is given, divide the difference by the difference in parts (7 : 9 with 40 more = 2 parts = 40 → 1 part = 20).

Checklist: find 1 part → work out exactly what is asked (other part / total / difference / money). Don’t stop at 1 part.

5Direct & inverse proportion

Always three steps: write the equation with k → find k from the given pair → substitute.

StatementEquationk =
y ∝ xy = kxy ÷ x
y ∝ x²y = kx²y ÷ x²
y ∝ x³y = kx³y ÷ x³
y ∝ √xy = k√xy ÷ √x
y ∝ 1/xy = k/xy × x
y ∝ 1/x²y = k/x²y × x²

Worked: y ∝ x² with y = 24 when x = 2 → k = 24/4 = 6 → y = 6x² → x = 4 gives y = 96.

“What happens when … is halved?” reason from the power: F ∝ 1/d², so halving d makes d² a quarter as big and F four times as big.

“Which relationship fits the table?” compute y ÷ x, y ÷ x², y ÷ x³, y ÷ √x (or y × x, y × x²) and pick the one that stays constant.

Past-paper practice — 4 real exam questions on recurring decimals

Straight from the actual Edexcel IGCSE Mathematics A papers (spec 4MA1). Do them on paper with full working — these are the “show that” style where the algebra earns the marks. Printable questions-only version: practice.pdf (questions only, room to write)
P1

Show that 0.254 = 14/55

2 marks

2019 Paper 1H, Q15 · real past paper, spec 4MA1

Show answer & working
Front: 2 (1 digit) → 0  |  Block: 54 (2 digits) → 99 → denominator 990
Top: 254 − 2 = 252
x       = 0.25454…
100x    = 25.454…
100x − x = 99x = 25.2
x = 25.2/99 = 252/990 = 14/55   (÷18)
Mark scheme: M1 for two recurring decimals that subtract to a whole number or terminating decimal (25.2 or 252), A1 for completion to 14/55 — algebra must be shown.
P2

Show that 0.2813 = 557/1980

2 marks

2021 Paper 1H, Q16 · real past paper, spec 4MA1

Show answer & working
Dots over 1 and 3 → block = 13; in front = 28 → 0s: 00, 9s: 99 → denominator 9900
Top: 2813 − 28 = 2785
x        = 0.281313…
100x     = 28.1313…
10000x   = 2813.13…
10000x − 100x = 9900x = 2785
x = 2785/9900 = 557/1980   (÷5)
Mark scheme also accepts 1000x = 281.313… with 10x = 2.81313… → 990x = 278.5 → 557/1980 — it only needs two recurring decimals that subtract to a whole number or terminating decimal.
P3

0.4ẋ as a fraction, in terms of x

3 marks

2021 Oct Paper 1H, Q18 · real past paper, spec 4MA1

Show answer & working
The dot is over the x only, so 0.4ẋ = 0.4xxxx… (only x repeats).
y       = 0.4xxxx…
10y     = 4.xxxx…
100y    = 4x.xxxx…        ← 4x here is the two-digit number 40 + x
100y − 10y = 90y = (40 + x) − 4 = 36 + x
y = (36 + x)/90
Check with x = 5: (36 + 5)/90 = 41/90 = 0.45555… = 0.45 ✓
Mark scheme: A1 (36 + x)/90 oe, dependent on two correct recurring-decimal expressions being subtracted; working required.
P4

Show that 0.612 = 101/165

2 marks

2025 Paper 1HR, Q16 · real past paper, spec 4MA1

Show answer & working
Dots over 1 and 2 → block = 12; in front = 6 → 0s: 0, 9s: 99 → denominator 990
Top: 612 − 6 = 606
x        = 0.6121212…
10x      = 6.121212…
1000x    = 612.121212…
1000x − 10x = 990x = 606
x = 606/990 = 101/165   (÷6)

Direct & inverse proportion

Three steps every time: write y = k(…), find k, substitute.

y is directly proportional to x²

3 marks
Show answer & working
y = kx². 24 = k × 2² = 4k → k = 6 → y = 6x².
x = 4: y = 6 × 16 = 96

H varies directly to the cube of c

Show answer & working
H = kc³. 40 = k × 2³ = 8k → k = 5.
(a) H = 5c³
(b) c = 5: H = 5 × 125 = 625
(c) H = 5000: c³ = 1000 → c = 10
Q6

Force inversely proportional to d²

3+1+1+1
Show answer & working
F = k/d². 50 = k/2² = k/4 → k = 50 × 4 = 200.
(a) F = 200/d²
(b) d = 10: F = 200/100 = 2 N
(c) F = 8: d² = 200/8 = 25 → d = 5 cm
(d) d halved → d² becomes ¼ as big, so F becomes 4 times as large.

4 marks

Degree of accuracy (bounds)

Bounds = value ± ½ of the stated accuracy. For calculations, big top / small bottom.

Table 105 cm to the nearest cm

1+1
Show answer & working
Half the accuracy = 0.5 cm.
(a) smallest = 105 − 0.5 = 104.5 cm
(b) largest = 105 + 0.5 = 105.5 cm
Error interval 104.5 ≤ L < 105.5

Q21

Lower bound of v = u + at

4 marks
Show answer & working
u = 2.4 (2 s.f.) → LB 2.35  |  a = 12 (2 s.f.) → LB 11.5  |  t = 5 (1 s.f.) → LB 4.5
v = 2.35 + 11.5 × 4.5 = 2.35 + 51.75 = 54.15 m/s

3 marks
